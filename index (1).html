02 · Nafplio

Make Nafplio your Peloponnese base.

For a first trip, this is the easy choice. It’s beautiful, compact, good at night, and puts Epidaurus and Mycenae within very easy reach.

Where to stay

Stay inside the Old Town.

There are plenty of small boutique hotels that are nice, so you don’t need to over-optimize. One place I’ve stayed at is Aethra Boutique Rooms, which I’m happy to recommend as a reference point.

03 · The ancient sites

Two places I would not skip.

Think of Epidaurus and Mycenae as historical places to visit while you’re based in Nafplio — not as separate stops where you need to stay. Epidaurus can also fit naturally on the drive between Athens and Nafplio.

Historical visit · near Nafplio

Epidaurus

This is the iconic theater — arguably the most prestigious stage in Greece. In spring you’re not going for a performance; the big productions are during the summer festival. It’s still absolutely worth seeing the theater and sanctuary.

Easy to visit from Nafplio, or stop there while driving between Athens and Nafplio.
Historical visit · very close to Nafplio

Mycenae

The legendary kingdom of Agamemnon, leader of the Greeks in the Trojan War. The citadel, royal tombs and museum make the story much more tangible than you might expect.

Allow roughly 1½–2 hours for the site + tombs + museum, then return to Nafplio.

04 · Bigger extensions

Olympia and Hydra are bigger trips worth considering.

These are not quick historical outings like Epidaurus or Mycenae. Each adds real travel time and may be worth an overnight stay. If time allows, you can absolutely include both.

History + mountain villages

Olympia

It’s a meaningful detour, but it’s farther: think 2½+ hours of driving from Nafplio. If you do it, I’d consider spending a night there rather than forcing it into a day trip.

The scenic version: take the more winding route through villages like Stemnitsa, Dimitsana and Vytina. Then head back toward Athens from Olympia on your last day.

Island trip

Hydra

Hydra is extremely beautiful and wonderfully simple: essentially one compact harbor town you explore on foot, and visitors don’t use cars on the island.

From Nafplio: you can also do Hydra as a day trip. Drive to Metochi, leave the car on the mainland, and take the roughly 20-minute water taxi across to Hydra. Spend the day walking around the town and harbor, then return to Metochi and drive back to Nafplio.

From Athens: another option is to take the ferry from Piraeus to Hydra and potentially spend the night there. Instead of returning to Athens by ferry, you can continue by water taxi to Metochi and then drive on toward Nafplio. The trick is the car: you’ll need to arrange a way to pick up a rental car around Metochi and return it later in Athens. A friend did exactly this this summer, so it is possible — I’d just arrange the rental logistics in advance rather than assume you can pick up a car on arrival.
